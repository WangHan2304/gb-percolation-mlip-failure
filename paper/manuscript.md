# Grain-boundary network percolation meets machine-learned grain-boundary energies: blind prediction of intergranular failure susceptibility with quantified uncertainty

---

## Abstract

Intergranular failure follows a percolation narrative: a connected path of susceptible boundaries must span before cracking proceeds. We present an auditable pipeline ranking intergranular failure susceptibility under a fixed equivalent-damage rule for Cu, Ni and Fe: machine-learned boundary energies decorate Voronoi microstructures with percolation descriptors, crystal-plasticity simulations supply failure labels, and grouped ablations, a twenty-microstructure blind test and an error-propagation audit quantify what the model knows. Topology alone blind-predicts percolation at AUC 0.774 (95% CI 0.62–0.90); the full random forest (RF-TG) reaches 0.799 and the linear baseline (Lin-TG) 0.825 — overlapping intervals: parity within present uncertainty; strain regression reaches *R*² 0.26–0.38. A decoupled control removing γ from the damage strength drops the energy-only channel to near-chance (CV AUC 0.584) while topology barely moves (0.799 → 0.787): part of the energy advantage is *constructed* by the label rule. Within this pipeline, potential-level perturbation is absorbed, not amplified: 7.0% of flips stay below the label's cross-potential instability (10.8%); propagation gains remain below unity. It yields relative rankings under a fixed synthetic-label rule; marginal conformal coverage holds, conditional coverage remains open under the frozen protocol; sensitivity analysis quantifies the cost of closing this gap: 2–4× per-metal calibration rows and ~25% wider intervals.

**Keywords**: grain boundary; percolation; machine-learned interatomic potentials; intergranular failure; conformal prediction; error propagation; crystal plasticity

## 1 Introduction

Grain-boundary engineering (GBE) — the design of boundary networks to control intergranular fracture [1] — rests on a percolation idea [2]: microstructural failure along boundaries is not a single-boundary property but a connectivity property — a connected path of susceptible boundaries must span the specimen before intergranular cracking proceeds systemically. This narrative is well established experimentally, notably in austenitic stainless steels where Σ3-boundary networks interrupt damage paths and improve resistance [3,4]. What the experimental programme has generally lacked is a predictive, quantitative counterpart: given a boundary-energy spectrum and a network graph, *which* microstructure fails first, and how confidently?

Two mature computational ingredients now make such a model conceivable. First, machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) such as chgnet [5] and MACE-MP [6] — products of the now-mature machine-learning approach to molecular and materials science [7] — evaluate grain-boundary energies at DFT-like cost, and recent audits have begun quantifying their accuracy across hundreds of boundary configurations [8]. Second, graph representations of structure have become standard in materials property prediction (crystal graph convolutional neural networks [9]), and graph and percolation statistics of boundary networks are cheap to compute. What is missing — the gap this paper occupies — is the *composition*: boundary energy × network topology → failure label → blind machine-learning test, with uncertainty quantification (UQ) and an error-propagation audit end to end. The nearest neighbours stop short: grain-level fatigue indicator GNNs treat boundaries as graph edges but target fatigue indicators rather than percolative failure [10]; MLIP boundary-energy evaluations stop at energy accuracy [8]; GBE percolation studies remain experimental [3].

We organise the gap as three research questions:

- **RQ1** — To what extent do topological statistics of the non-twin boundary network (connectivity, winding thresholds, cluster statistics) determine intergranular failure susceptibility, relative to the boundary energies themselves?
- **RQ2** — Can a model driven by MLIP boundary energies and network-graph features blind-predict failure susceptibility for unseen microstructures (varying grain count, twin density, twin-length fraction), and where is the applicability-domain boundary?
- **RQ3** — How much does MLIP boundary-energy error propagate along "energy → network features → failure prediction" — do topological features absorb or amplify atomic-level error?

Our contributions are four-fold: (1) an *audited* boundary-energy spectrum for three metals × three potentials with an explicit two-layer error structure (Sec. 3.1); (2) a microstructure library of 200 periodic Voronoi RVEs with energy-decorated percolation descriptors and 360 CPFEM-derived failure labels under a transparent equivalent-damage model (Secs. 3.2–3.3); (3) a leakage-controlled ablation and blind test with split-conformal UQ and a k-nearest-neighbour applicability domain (Secs. 3.4–3.5), together with a damage-law robustness audit that re-labels the same stress histories under alternative rules and quantifies the label's γ-coupling (Sec. 3.7); and (4) an error-propagation audit whose perturbation source is the *measured* inter-potential spread rather than an assumed noise model (Sec. 3.6). The full pipeline is summarised in Fig. 1.

## 2 Methods

### 2.1 Boundary-energy spectra (E1)

Grain-boundary energies come from a single code path that constructs a boundary without crystallographic table look-up: a slab of lattice cut along (*hkl*), mirrored across the mid-plane (a 180°[*hkl*] rotation, i.e. the classic Σ3/Σ5/Σ9/Σ11 equivalences for (111)/(112)/(210,310)/(221)/(113)), rigidly translated over a 1/4-cell grid of in-plane shift states and four relative-height states. Each boundary is a *single*-GB slab with the out-of-plane periodicity switched off (vacuum ≥ 9 Å), and the energy is referenced against a same-thickness, same-termination free-surface slab so that the two surface terms cancel pairwise:

γ_GB = (E_GB − N·e_atom)/A − (E_ref − N_ref·e_atom)/A.

Slabs keep ≥ 6 layers per grain (thinner stacks mis-stack), an in-plane extent ≥ 9 Å, and a half-block thickness ≥ 8 Å. Candidates are pre-screened at interatomic distance ≥ 2.0 Å, relaxed with FIRE (*f*max = 0.02 eV Å⁻¹, ≤ 150 steps — a hard cap against pathological structures), with a *z*-scale refinement pass (×1.00/×1.05) absorbing GB and surface relaxation. The grid comprises 17 (metal, GB-family) cells × 3 potentials = 51 computations; 50 passed acceptance (relaxed *d*min = 2.21–2.55 Å; lattice constants Cu 3.615–3.626 Å, Ni 3.502–3.513 Å, Fe 2.849–2.858 Å) and one (Ni Σ5(310) under mace-mp0-medium) collapsed catastrophically during relaxation and was gated out as a qualitative failure, not a quantitative deviation. Potentials: chgnet [5], mace-mp0-medium, mace-mp0-small [6]. An internal-consistency check passes: the geometrically equivalent Fe Σ5(310)/(130) planes return bit-identical energies under each potential.

### 2.2 Microstructure library and network percolation features (E2)

Two hundred periodic 2D microstructures (256×256 pixels, unit cell) are generated by periodic Voronoi tessellation [11]: 40–200 grain seeds per sample, each assigned a random parent orientation; twin prevalence drawn per sample (probability *U*(0, 0.65)) and parents grouped into *n*/8 spatially compact clusters by a coarse seed layer, so that twin relationships (60° about ⟨111⟩, one of four equivalent axes) can form on shared cluster boundaries. The boundary network is the periodic 4-neighbour adjacency graph stored as an edge table (pixel length, wrap-around flags, edge type ∈ {twin, twin-related, general}).

Each edge receives an energy sampled from the E1 pools of the given (metal, potential): twin edges draw from the Σ3(111) pool, all others from the remaining family pool. The draw stream is a single per-sample generator (`default_rng(42 + sample)`) shared with microstructure generation, a property we exploit in Sec. 2.5. Six topology features (grain count, twin probability, edge count, mean degree, clustering coefficient, twin-length fraction) are potential-invariant by construction; six energy features (mean and standard deviation of γ, the winding threshold γ_perc in absolute and normalised form, and two failed-cluster statistics) are computed per (metal, potential).

γ_perc is the *maximin cycle bottleneck*: edges are added in descending energy order with a union-find that tracks integer wrap-around offsets; the first non-contractible (winding) loop appears when the edge at γ = γ_perc completes it, so the supra-threshold subnetwork {γ ≥ γ_perc} is exactly the lowest threshold whose high-energy subnetwork winds around the periodic cell — the energy scale at which high-energy boundaries form a connected path across the cell. The failed-cluster curve sweeps a threshold θ over 40 values, unions edges with γ > θ, and records the largest failed-cluster fraction; its normalised area (AUC) and its value at θ = ⟨γ⟩ complete the feature set. As a sanity check, mean degree is 5.96 ≈ 6, as required by Euler's relation for 2D tessellations.

### 2.3 Intergranular failure labels (E3)

Forty microstructures are selected from the 200 by greedy max-min coverage in (grain count, twin probability, twin-length fraction) space. Each 256² grid is majority-vote downsampled 4× to 64² (grain and edge survival ≥ 99%), extruded to a columnar 3D RVE, and run through DAMASK (20240629, spectral solver) [12,13]: one crystal-plasticity simulation per (microstructure, metal) — 120 runs — with phenomenological power-law slip (parameters from the DAMASK example set and literature; Cu initial CRSS polycrystal-calibrated to ≈ 12 MPa), uniaxial tension along *x* (Ḟ = 1.5×10⁻³ s⁻¹, *t* = 40 s → ε_xx = 0.06, 40 increments, 21 outputs at Δε = 0.003) and traction-free lateral faces.

Post-processing recovers per-boundary normal-stress histories σ_n(ε) (Cauchy stress from σ = P·Fᵀ/det F, averaged over each interface's cells; edges lost to downsampling, < 1%, median-imputed at matching times). Failure is then integrated by an *equivalent damage model* that reuses the E1 spectrum — the same CPFEM run yields three label sets, one per potential:

σ_c,e = k_{metal,pot} · (γ_e + γ₀(metal))^p,   dD_e = (⟨σ_n⟩₊ / σ_c,e)² dε_xx,   m = 2,

with permanent failure at D ≥ 1. The offset γ₀ (Cu 22, Ni 70, Fe 0 mJ m⁻² — Cu set at the experimental coherent-twin level 21–24 mJ m⁻² [14], Ni at the ab initio twin level ≈74 mJ m⁻² [15]) absorbs the pathologically low/negative twin values documented in Sec. 3.1; the exponent p = 0.65 and gain κ = 0.7 are calibration constants: k is set per (metal, potential) as κ · median_runs[σ̄_net(ε_ref = 0.05) / ⟨(γ+γ₀)^p⟩], placing typical microstructures in the percolation transition band within ε ≤ 0.06. A first version with a linear coupling σ_c ∝ γ transferred the spectrum's 30–60× dynamic range directly into strength ratios: coherent twins failed at the very first output step and 57% of percolation events piled up at ε = 0.003 — a degenerate, unlearnable label distribution. The sub-linear, offset coupling removes this (first-step pile-up 0%) and is reported as an *equivalent* model: labels measure relative susceptibility under one fixed damage rule, not absolute failure strains.

Percolation is detected exactly as in E2 — a winding loop of failed edges about the *y*-direction means the crack plane ⊥ *x* spans the RVE. Labels per microstructure × metal × potential: the percolation verdict within ε_max = 0.06 (binary), ε_fail at percolation (censored at ε_max otherwise), and the failed GB length fraction. The base label set contains 360 labels = 40 × 3 metals × 3 potentials (155 percolated); the E4b extension (Sec. 2.4) adds 108 test-only labels for twelve further microstructures under the frozen per-(metal, potential) gains, so the repository holds 468 labelled rows in total, of which the 108 extension rows never enter training, calibration, model selection or τ.

### 2.4 Blind machine-learning protocol (E4)

Rows are split by *microstructure group* so that no organisation leaks across sets: 24/8/8 microstructures (seed 20261023) → 216/72/72 rows (train/cal/blind; blind percolation rate 55.6%). Feature sets: **T** (the six topology features), **G** (the six energy features), **TG** (all twelve); a metal one-hot is always included; the potential identity is deliberately *not* a feature — potential-induced label differences should be absorbed by the γ features, which is exactly the learnable signal under test. Models: random forest (500 trees, min leaf 2) [16] and a linear baseline (standardised logistic regression / ridge, α = 10). Cross-validation for the ablation is 5-fold × 4-repeat StratifiedGroupKFold (groups = microstructure) — grouped folds are required whenever rows are correlated within groups, since row-wise splitting yields optimistically biased error estimates [17,18]. Uncertainty: split-conformal intervals [19,20] at α = 0.1 (finite-sample corrected quantile, *higher* method) for the regression target; classification is scored by AUC without intervals. Intervals are computed on the calibration groups' 36 non-censored rows (of the 72 calibration rows); the blind-set extension changed neither the models nor this calibration set. Applicability domain: each row's distance d₅ to its five nearest training neighbours in the standardised full-feature space; τ = 95th percentile of calibration d₅ (= 3.53), rows beyond τ are flagged out-of-domain (OOD); the k-nearest-neighbour construction follows standard applicability-domain practice [21,22].

After the model and all hyperparameters were frozen, the blind set was extended by twelve further microstructures, chosen from the 160 library microstructures outside the base 40. Their identifiers were fixed in code before any labels existed, and the set spans the library's full grain-count range (40–200 grains; twin probability 0.17–0.55, twin-length fraction 0.15–0.32); none was used for training, calibration, model selection or τ. The twelve were re-simulated under the identical E3 protocol (36 new CPFEM runs, three metals each; fixed per-(metal, potential) damage gains, no re-calibration) and added to the test set only, giving 20 blind microstructures / 180 rows (100 non-censored for regression). The train/calibration split and every grouped-CV number above are unchanged. All blind metrics are reported with 95% confidence intervals from a nonparametric bootstrap [23] that resamples *microstructure groups* with replacement (rows of a sampled group enter together, preserving the cross-metal/cross-potential correlation; B = 2000, percentile interval; bootstrap draws in which one class is absent are discarded for AUC — none occurred in the present audit (all 2000 draws valid)). Code: `ml/e4b_blindtest_ext.py`.

The primary blind set throughout this paper is this twenty-microstructure / 180-row set. The propagation audit (E5, Sec. 2.5) and the damage-law audit (E6, Sec. 3.7) were executed on the original frozen eight-microstructure *audit* set — run before the extension and never re-benchmarked against it. Their headline numbers (e.g. the replayed AUC 0.8508) are quoted on that audit set, whereas all main results use the twenty-group set.

### 2.5 Error-propagation audit (E5, RQ3)

The perturbation source is *measured*, not assumed: the three potential pools of E1 are three realisations of MLIP boundary-energy error (Sec. 3.1). For perturbation level p ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}, every edge independently resamples its energy from a *different* potential's same-family pool with probability p (one alternative potential per trial). The unperturbed γ vectors are reproduced by replaying E2's random stream exactly (`default_rng(42 + sample)` shared with grid generation). Across all 40 labelled microstructures the replayed mean γ agrees with the stored feature table to ≤ 7×10⁻⁵ (pure rounding). Features are recomputed, predictions re-issued with E4-identical models (the unperturbed blind AUC on the original eight-group *audit* set reproduces 0.8508 exactly — this audit set, not the primary twenty-group set, underlies E5), and 8 Monte-Carlo trials per level pool the drift. Topology features are invariant by construction and form the structural zero control; the **T**-only model must therefore show exactly zero drift. The propagation gain is G = median relative prediction drift ÷ median relative γ-feature drift (G < 1 ⇒ the prediction layer absorbs input error).

## 3 Results

### 3.1 The spectra have a two-layer error structure (Fig. 2, Table 1)

Across all three metals, potentials disagree in *kind*, not only in degree. At coherent-twin-type boundaries (low-lying energy scale; literature 21–24 mJ m⁻² for Cu [14] and 41–90 for Ni [15,24]), the potentials underestimate and can change sign: Cu Σ3(111) computes as −14.4 (chgnet), +5.4, +7.8; Ni Σ3(111) as +12.7, +1.4, −0.4. At high-angle boundaries the magnitude is broadly right but the spread is quantitative and reaches up to 3×: the two MACE sizes put Cu Σ3(112) at 559–579, inside the ab initio window (547–684 [15,25]), while chgnet overshoots at 1002; Ni is systematically high — Σ5(310) at 2234–3840 against a 1218–1262 window [26,27]; and Fe Σ5(210) spans 771–2492 across potentials against 1090–2000 [26,28]. One cell is the opposite of scattered: Fe Σ3(112) returns 113.7/177.3/176.5 under the three potentials — the tightest inter-potential spread in the library (1.6×) — yet all three sit below the ab initio window (340–510 [26]), agreeing with one another while sharing a common low bias: a construction-level offset rather than potential noise. Figure 2 plots the full spectra; the twin-layer sign flips and the Fe consensus anchor are annotated.

### 3.2 Network features carry a percolation signature — and potential identity (Fig. 3)

Pooling all 1800 (microstructure × metal × potential) rows, twin-length fraction correlates positively but modestly with the normalised winding threshold (Pearson *r* = 0.17; per metal: Cu 0.23, Ni 0.25, Fe −0.05). Aggregating γ features per microstructure — removing the potential-level scatter — raises the correlation to *r* = 0.46, i.e. the organisational signal is real but is partly masked, not created, by potential-level variation. That variation is itself informative: for the *same* microstructure, γ_perc under different potentials separates by a median factor 1.5× (max 3.4×). The energy channel therefore carries a measurable fingerprint of which potential produced it — the raw material for RQ3.

### 3.3 Failure labels are distributed and only partially percolated (Fig. 4)

Of 360 labels, 155 (43.1%) percolate within ε ≤ 0.06; failure strains span 0.009–0.06 with median 0.036 and no pile-up at the first output step (v2 calibration succeeded). Percolation counts by metal: Cu 64, Ni 56, Fe 35 (censored fractions 47%, 53%, 71%). At the moment of percolation the failed boundary length fraction has median 0.128 — failure is a *partial*-failure connectivity event (a winding path of damaged boundaries), not wholesale fracture, which is the physically right regime for a percolation label. Percolated strains separate visibly by potential within each metal (Fig. 4, left), confirming that the potential identity leaks into labels through σ_c(γ) as designed — and setting up the ablation.

### 3.4 Ablation and blind test (Fig. 5, Fig. 6, Table 2)

Cross-validated (5×4 grouped; mean ± fold-to-fold sd over the 20 folds, not a confidence interval): classification AUC rises T 0.799 ± 0.082 → G 0.816 ± 0.063 → TG 0.829 ± 0.064 (random forest); the linear baseline reaches 0.844 ± 0.055 on TG. Regression *R*²: T 0.247, G 0.403, TG 0.424 (RF; linear 0.263/0.362/0.295). Both channels carry signal, and the full feature set has the highest mean under grouped CV — a point-estimate ordering, since the fold-to-fold sd's overlap.

On the twenty blind microstructures (180 rows; 100 non-censored), *discrimination transfers*. The CV spread and the blind bootstrap CI are different quantities — fold-to-fold variability of the fitting procedure versus sampling uncertainty over blind groups — and are not directly compared. Percolation AUC = 0.799 (TG-RF, 95% group-bootstrap CI 0.66–0.91) and 0.825 (TG-Lin, 0.71–0.92), against 0.774 (T-RF) and 0.767 (G-RF) — every interval lies well above the 0.5 chance line; TG has the highest point estimate, but its group-bootstrap interval overlaps those of T and G, so the blind set does not resolve a ranking among feature sets. T and G trade places (0.774 vs 0.767) far inside those same overlapping intervals, so the two single channels are indistinguishable on the blind set. The original eight-microstructure point estimate (AUC 0.851 TG-RF, Fig. 5; plotted as a grey cross reference in Fig. 6) sits inside its own group-bootstrap interval (0.70–0.97) and inside the twenty-group interval: with only eight groups the point estimate was noisier than it looked, in the optimistic direction here. *Magnitude also transfers once the blind set is large enough to measure it*: ε_fail regression gives *R*² = 0.26–0.38 across models (G-RF 0.377, CI 0.09–0.59, and G-Lin 0.365, CI 0.09–0.54 — the two G-feature intervals exclude zero, while the T- and TG-intervals touch it at the lower edge, e.g. TG-RF 0.299, CI −0.07–0.56), with MAE 0.0084–0.0092 strain units. The forest-versus-linear asymmetry seen on eight groups (forest apparently worse) does not survive the larger sample: the two models' intervals overlap throughout — the honest reading is *parity within present uncertainty*, not a ranking. On the twelve purely added microstructures alone (ext12 subset, coloured separately in the Fig. 6 scatter), the full model reaches AUC 0.770 (0.57–0.94) and *R*² 0.44 (point estimate; group-bootstrap CI −0.12–0.74 touches zero at twelve groups) — a genuine extrapolation set the frozen model never saw during any stage of fitting. This is the substantive RQ2 answer: the *verdict* (does this microstructure percolate within 6% strain?) blind-predicts at AUC ≈ 0.80 (CI excluding chance), the failure strain is recoverable at *R*² ≈ 0.3 but its interval still touches zero — both numbers now carry quantified uncertainty rather than single-group point estimates.

### 3.5 Marginal coverage holds, conditional coverage remains open; the domain flag fires rarely (Fig. 7)

Regression split-conformal 90% intervals achieve blind coverage 0.86–0.99 across all six model/feature-set combinations on the twenty-microstructure set (TG-RF: 0.86, CI 0.74–0.96, at half-width 0.017 strain; TG-Lin: 0.99, 0.97–1.00, at 0.025 — mildly conservative, as expected from a 36-row calibration set) — consistent with the nominal 0.90; the calibration set's composition is unchanged by the blind-set extension. This is *marginal* coverage pooled over metals and potentials; conditional coverage is not established. Split by metal, TG-RF gives 0.74 (Cu, n = 43; 95% Wilson interval 0.60–0.85, excluding the 0.90 nominal), 0.92 (Ni, n = 39; 0.80–0.97) and 1.00 (Fe, n = 18; 0.82–1.00) — the linear baseline gives 0.98–1.00 — so with 18–43 rows per metal the estimates are coarse, but the spread (Cu under-coverage under the forest, Fe over-coverage) is exactly why conditional coverage is flagged as open rather than claimed; the calibration set itself is metal-balanced (12 non-censored rows per metal), so the Cu shortfall is a genuine distribution question, not a calibration-composition artefact. An exploratory per-metal (Mondrian) recalibration does not repair the spread: with 12 calibration rows per metal the within-metal 90% quantile is the maximum residual, so for TG-RF Cu barely moves (0.74 → 0.74) while Fe drops (1.00 → 0.61) and pooled coverage falls (0.86 → 0.80) — metal stratification cannot remove the group-level shift between calibration and blind microstructures, so the binding constraint is per-metal calibration sample size, not the conformal variant (`results/e4c_mondrian.csv`). To size that constraint, an expanded-calibration sensitivity run (`results/e4d_cal_sensitivity.csv`) augments the frozen 36-row pool with five-fold out-of-fold residuals from the 24 training groups (36 → 115 non-censored rows; per metal 12 → 48/41/26 for Cu/Ni/Fe). Models, split and blind set are untouched (these residuals never enter the frozen protocol; they exist only to size the calibration-sample constraint). Pooled over metals the extra rows change nothing — TG-RF blind coverage stays 0.86 and Cu stays 0.74 — confirming again that the Cu shortfall is a calibration-versus-blind distribution question rather than a pooled-sample-size artefact.

The per-metal 90% quantile, however, is no longer the within-metal maximum: Mondrian coverage rises from 0.80 to 0.94 (95% group-bootstrap CI 0.87–0.99), Cu recovering 0.74 → 0.91 and Fe 0.61 → 1.00, at a half-width cost of 0.017 → 0.021 strain (about a quarter wider). The linear baseline, in turn, sheds its mild conservatism once pooled over the expanded set (coverage 0.99 → 0.95 at half-width 0.025 → 0.021). The conditional-coverage gap is thus quantitatively closeable — at roughly two to four times the per-metal calibration rows and about a quarter more interval width — so the binding constraint is per-metal calibration sample size, a resource limit of the frozen protocol rather than a defect of split conformal. Because the added residuals are out-of-fold predictions on the training groups rather than an independent holdout, we report this as sensitivity analysis only, and the frozen-protocol numbers above remain the ones claimed. Folding them into the frozen protocol would compromise the blind-set purity that the protocol was designed to protect. The applicability domain (τ = 3.53) flags 6 of 180 blind rows as OOD (three in each of the original and added subsets); the flagged rows span all three potentials' regimes rather than one corner of feature space. Residuals do not blow up with d₅ in the blind set (Fig. 7, the original eight microstructures at full row resolution; the extended set is not re-plotted against d₅ — per-row d₅ and domain flags live in `results/e4b_prediction_audit.csv`, and the two OOD rows among the 100 regression rows are marked in Fig. 6) — with six OOD rows we read the flag as a *guard rail* whose false-negative rate cannot yet be estimated, not as a proven residual predictor.

### 3.6 Error propagation: absorbed within the pipeline, not amplified (Fig. 8, Table 3)

Perturbing every edge to a different potential's pool (p = 1) moves the γ features by median relative 18% (mean γ), 24% (γ std), 19%/15% (γ_perc absolute/relative), 11% (cluster AUC) and 3% (failed fraction at mean) — while all topology features move by exactly 0, and both the p = 0 control and the T-only model show machine-zero drift throughout (self-checks pass).

At the prediction layer, the full model's binary verdict flips on 7.0% of rows under full perturbation (4.8%, 6.6%, 6.9% at p = 0.25/0.5/0.75) — *below* the label's own cross-potential instability (13/120 = 10.8% of censoring verdicts change when the label itself is regenerated under another potential). The predictor is thus more stable than its supervision. Regression predictions move by median |Δε̂| = 0.0030 at p = 1 — about one third of the primary blind MAE (0.0084–0.0092; the original eight-group audit MAE is slightly larger, 0.0095–0.0114, computed row-wise from `results/prediction_audit.csv`) — and the propagation gain decays monotonically with p: G = 0.76, 0.49, 0.42, 0.39 (all < 1). The prediction layer *compresses* relative input error; the topology-only channel admits none. RQ3's answer: within this pipeline, atomic-level energy error is absorbed at every stage it passes through, and the features that never see atoms are immune by construction.

### 3.7 Label robustness under alternative damage laws (E6)

The labels of Sec. 3.3 rest on one equivalent-damage rule; two re-labelling checks ask how much of the paper's conclusions ride on that choice. Both reuse the same 360 CPFEM stress histories, so no new simulation is involved. (The blind figures in this section are quoted on the original eight-group *audit* set — the re-labellings predate the Sec. 2.4 extension, so they are compared against the 0.851 baseline of that set rather than the twenty-group primary value; they are deliberately not re-benchmarked on the extended set.) For reference, the same TG-RF model scores 0.799 (0.66–0.91) on the twenty-group primary set; the eight-group values here are used only for like-for-like comparison with the pre-extension audits. Table 4 summarises all five label variants.

**Alternative coupled laws.** Re-labelling under linear accumulation (m = 1), Griffith-like weaker coupling (p = 0.5) and linear stronger coupling (p = 1.0) uses a single per-metal gain on σ_c (0.22–3.65, solved by log-bisection) so that every variant reproduces the baseline per-metal percolation counts (Cu 64 / Ni 56 / Fe 35) — count matching is a diagnostic normalisation that isolates the *shape* of the law from a trivial strength scale, never tuned against predictive performance — and first-step pile-up stays at 0% throughout. Under each variant the models are retrained from scratch with identical hyperparameters, seeds and group splits, so the comparison asks whether the conclusions survive a changed label rather than whether a fixed predictor does. Verdicts agree with the baseline on 97.2–100% of rows; for m = 1 the agreement is exactly 1.000 with all AUCs identical, because m enters only as the stress-ratio exponent of the damage-accumulation rate (D ∝ ∫(σ_n/σ_c)^m dt) — halving it shifts damage timing without changing the realised connectivity pattern — while ε_fail ordering is preserved throughout (Spearman ρ 0.976–0.991). The ML conclusions hold: grouped CV AUC (TG-RF) 0.819–0.829 versus 0.829 baseline, blind AUC 0.827–0.855 versus 0.851, with the grouped-CV T < G < TG ablation order unchanged and the full model still best blind.

**Decoupled control (γ removed from the strength).** With σ_c constant per metal and percolation counts approximately re-matched (63/57/36 — a constant per-metal strength cannot hit the baseline counts exactly), labels agree with the baseline on only 64.7% of rows and the ε_fail ranking collapses to ρ = 0.119 — a direct measure of how much of the label is a function of γ. The G-only channel then loses most of its signal (grouped CV AUC 0.816 → 0.584; blind 0.807 → 0.542, i.e. chance) while T-only barely moves in grouped CV (0.799 → 0.787); even the full-feature blind AUC falls from 0.851 to 0.663 (Table 4). On the 72-row blind audit set every decoupled random-forest channel drops relative to the baseline (T 0.823 → 0.639, G 0.807 → 0.542, TG 0.851 → 0.663), so the blind columns of Table 4 are read ordinally — G < T < TG, the CV ordering preserved — while the level comparison behind "G to chance, T stable" rests on grouped CV; the residual CV TG > T gap (0.810 vs 0.787) is within fold-to-fold sd and is not interpreted as signal. The standalone energy-channel advantage is thus largely *constructed* by the σ_c(γ) coupling — quantifying the RQ1 caveat of Sec. 4 — whereas the coupling-free topology channel is unchanged and remains the lower-bound estimate of the topological share.

**Twin-fraction proxy (a diagnostic under this rule, not an experimental GBE claim).** Spearman correlation of twin-length fraction with the percolation verdict is +0.326 pooled (p < 10⁻⁹) but splits by metal: Fe is −0.303 (p = 8 × 10⁻⁴), consistent with Σ3 networks interrupting damage paths [3], while Cu (+0.658) and Ni (+0.587) are opposite to the classical narrative. Two features of the label generator account for the signs. The rule inverts physical intuition by construction: σ_c ∝ (γ + γ₀)^p makes *low*-γ boundaries the *weak* ones — a deliberate simplification of the label generator — so the Cu/Ni positive correlation follows directly from the rule's energy channel (more twins → more low-γ Σ3 edges → more low-σ_c edges → easier percolation). Fe, with the lowest percolation rate of the three metals (35/120, 71% censored), is consistent with a topology-dominated regime in which twin boundaries mainly *segment* the network rather than supply weak links — and a targeted probe confirms the negative sign does not come from the Fe anchor: raising γ₀(Fe) from 0 to the Cu/Ni values (22, 70) and re-running the full damage integration (re-sampled γ̃, re-calibrated k, percolation counts matched to 35 as in the E6 protocol) keeps ρ negative throughout (−0.303, −0.303, −0.299 for γ₀ = 0, 22, 70), and without count matching the Fe counts shift 35 → 28 → 10 while ρ stays negative (−0.303, −0.351, −0.253); the γ₀ = 0 run reproduces the published labels row by row, and Cu/Ni are untouched by construction (`results/e6_fe_gamma0_probe.csv`). This correlation is reported as a *diagnostic* of how the fixed equivalent-damage rule maps network structure onto failure — it is not evidence for or against classical GBE, and the metal dependence of the sign is precisely why every claim in this paper is framed as *relative susceptibility rankings under a fixed rule* rather than absolute GBE expectations.

## 4 Discussion

**RQ1 — topology versus energy.** Topology alone reaches AUC 0.799 / *R*² 0.247, so a substantial, coupling-free share of failure susceptibility lives in the graph; energy features add on top (TG > G in both targets), and energy's larger regression increment (0.247 → 0.403) must be read with one honest caveat: the labels themselves are generated through σ_c(γ) from the same E1 pools, so part of G's advantage is constructed, not discovered — and the decoupled-label control (Sec. 3.7) puts numbers on it: removing γ from the damage strength at approximately matched percolation flips 35% of verdicts, collapses the ε_fail ranking to ρ = 0.119, and drops the G-only channel to CV AUC 0.584 while T-only barely moves in grouped CV (0.799 → 0.787). T's performance is unaffected by that coupling and therefore stands as a *lower bound* on the topological share; TG's gain over G is the clean evidence that topology contributes beyond energy — the paper's joint-determination claim, with the two contributions disentangled rather than asserted.

**RQ2 — blind generalisation and its boundary.** Discrimination survives the shift to unseen microstructures (AUC 0.799, group-bootstrap CI 0.66–0.91 across twenty blind groups, well clear of chance), and magnitude regression is recoverable but less certain (*R*² 0.26–0.38; the full model's interval −0.07–0.56 touches zero). Three readings follow from the enlarged blind set: first, eight groups gave two noisy point estimates in opposite directions — 0.851 AUC was optimistic, 0.078 *R*² pessimistic, both inside the twenty-group intervals — which is the statistical argument for reporting groups-bootstrap intervals rather than single-split values; second, the linear baseline and the forest are at parity within present uncertainty on both targets, so the response looks close to low-dimensional/monotone rather than forest-dominated; third, the conformal layer is not decoration — intervals held nominal coverage (0.86–0.99) across the extended blind set exactly where point predictions carry wide intervals. The domain flag (6/180) is cheap and fires rarely at this sampling density; at larger scales its precision should be re-evaluated rather than assumed.

**RQ3 — the propagation audit.** Using measured inter-potential spread as the perturbation source avoids inventing a noise model, and the exact replay of the E2 random stream makes the baseline reproducible to rounding. Two structural results dominate: topological features cannot carry atomic error (zero by construction, verified to machine precision), and the model layer absorbs the remainder (G < 1 at every p, flips below the label's own instability). The audit is within-pipeline — perturbed features are scored against labels generated from unperturbed energies — so it measures *sensitivity of the trained predictor*, not end-to-end label regeneration; the latter is the natural extension and would likely widen the drift, though the label-side anchor (10.8%) already brackets it.

**2D versus 3D percolation.** γ_perc is a winding threshold defined on a periodic two-dimensional cell, and percolation thresholds and critical exponents belong to dimensionality-dependent universality classes [2] — the two-dimensional bond and site problems differ from their three-dimensional counterparts both in threshold values and in exponents, and so does the geometry of the spanning set. Two consequences follow for the transferability of γ_perc. The *form* of the descriptor carries over: the maximin cycle bottleneck is the energy-ordered connectivity statistic of a grain-boundary graph irrespective of embedding dimension, and a 3D network admits the same computation with wrapping in any of three directions. The *calibration* does not: the numerical threshold, the winding criterion adopted here and the percolation verdict itself are established in the 2D columnar universality class and would have to be re-established in 3D, where the spanning geometry and critical scale differ [2]. Our columnar, 2D-periodic RVEs realise exactly the geometry in which γ_perc is defined, which is consistent with the framing used throughout: every reported number is a relative ranking under a fixed 2D rule, not a portable threshold: a γ_perc value reported here should not be compared across studies that define the winding threshold in 3D.

**Relation to prior work.** Where FIP-GNN maps boundary graphs to fatigue indicators [10] and MLIP audits map potentials to energies [8], we compose spectrum → graph → label → blind test → propagation audit. MLIP error propagation into continuum-scale predictions poses the same question one level downstream; here the propagated quantity is a *feature within a learned predictor* rather than a downstream continuum field, closing the loop between the two narratives. On the fracture side, recent extended Rice–Thomson analyses resolve grain-boundary fracture criteria atomistically [29], hydrogen-assisted intergranular cracking is the canonical chemical route to intergranular failure [30], and MLIPs now target hydrogen-embrittlement mechanisms [31], but neither line — nor the MLIP audits [8] or the FIP-GNN [10] — composes energy spectrum, network topology and a validated failure label into a single blind-tested predictor; that composition is what this paper supplies, with UQ and an error-propagation audit attached.

**Limitations.** (i) Labels come from one equivalent-damage model, calibrated (κ, p, γ₀) rather than derived; claims are about *relative susceptibility rankings under a fixed rule* — the same protocol applied to any candidate microstructure — not absolute failure strains. Sec. 3.7 re-labels the same stress histories under alternative m/p laws and shows the rankings and ablation conclusions stable, and quantifies the γ-coupling via the decoupled control, but no phase-field or experimental cross-validation subset was run. The label is therefore a controlled synthetic target, not a validated GBE predictor: the Cu/Ni sign reversal in the twin-fraction proxy (Sec. 3.7) shows that the fixed rule does not reproduce classical GBE trends for all metals — it is a property of the equivalent-damage rule, not evidence against classical GBE — and it is precisely why all claims are framed as relative rankings under this rule and about the ML/UQ pipeline, not about absolute GBE physics; the paper is accordingly positioned as a methodological pipeline study rather than a physical-mechanism paper. (ii) RVEs are 2D-periodic and columnar, downsampled 4×; periodicity removes free-surface effects but 3D crack-front topology is absent. (iii) Microstructures are synthetic Voronoi statistics rather than reconstructed EBSD. (iv) Blind scale is twenty microstructures (180 rows, 100 non-censored for regression), which still leaves wide intervals — roughly ±0.10–0.14 on AUC and ±0.22–0.32 on *R*² — and conformal quantiles rest on 36 calibration rows whose composition (the original eight calibration groups) was not enlarged along with the test set; marginal coverage is established, conditional coverage remains open (Sec. 3.5, where an expanded-calibration sensitivity run quantifies the improvement space — per-metal Mondrian coverage 0.80 → 0.94 at intervals a quarter wider — without altering the frozen protocol); the out-of-fold residuals may be optimistically narrow relative to a genuinely independent calibration pool, so the 0.94 figure should be read as an upper bound on the achievable Mondrian coverage. (v) Literature anchors in Table 1 are cross-method windows (experimental, empirical-potential and ab initio values per cell), verified against the cited sources but still compilations spanning different methods; only three potentials sample the MLIP error distribution.

## 5 Conclusions

1. **Joint determination.** Intergranular failure susceptibility under a fixed damage rule is carried by both boundary-network topology and the boundary-energy spectrum; each channel predicts out of sample, and the full feature set is best under grouped cross-validation (AUC 0.829, *R*² 0.424). The energy channel's larger increment is partly constructed by the γ-dependent label rule rather than discovered — the topology-only result is immune to that coupling and therefore a lower bound on the topological share, while TG over G is the clean evidence that topology contributes beyond energy.
2. **Blind verdict, intervalled magnitude.** On twenty unseen microstructures — twelve added after the model was frozen — the percolation verdict blind-predicts at AUC 0.80 (RF-TG; 95% microstructure-group bootstrap CI 0.66–0.91; Lin-TG 0.825, CI 0.71–0.92 — overlapping, parity within present uncertainty) and ε_fail regression gives point-estimate *R*² 0.26–0.38 — but only the energy-feature intervals exclude zero, the topology and full-feature intervals still touch it at the lower edge, so the magnitude is recovered in point-estimate terms yet not established as a robust regression signal for the full feature set; split-conformal intervals keep nominal 90% *marginal* coverage (0.86–0.99) while per-metal coverage reveals Cu under-coverage (0.74) and conditional coverage remains open; 6/180 rows are flagged out of domain.
3. **Errors are absorbed within the pipeline.** Under full measured potential-level perturbation on the original eight-microstructure audit set, γ features drift 15–24%, predictions flip 7.0% (below the labels' own 10.8% cross-potential instability), regression moves by 0.003 strain, and propagation gain stays below unity (0.39–0.76); topological features are structurally immune.
