Cover letter — International Journal of Plasticity

Dear Editor,

We would like to submit our manuscript entitled "Grain-boundary network
percolation meets machine-learned grain-boundary energies: blind prediction
of intergranular failure susceptibility" for consideration as an original
research paper in the International Journal of Plasticity.

The question we address is one of failure criteria as much as of machine
learning: when a polycrystal's grain-boundary network is decorated with a
real, potential-dependent boundary-energy spectrum, what determines which
microstructure fails first in intergranular mode, and how far can that verdict
be trusted? Crystal plasticity supplies the labels — 360 periodic columnar
RVEs solved with the DAMASK spectral solver under a fixed, documented
equivalent-damage rule — while machine-learned boundary energies decorate the
same microstructures with percolation descriptors. The resulting ranking model
is then tested blind on twenty microstructures withheld from every stage of
fitting.

The point we believe is most relevant to this journal's readership is a
mechanics check on the failure criterion itself:

- The labels come from an equivalent-damage rule, not from a fracture-mechanics
  criterion. We therefore re-solve a 46-microstructure subset with an
  independent phase-field fracture engine (AT2, 138 runs) that is given
  nothing but the same boundary-energy map. It recovers the direction of the
  susceptibility ordering, but arrests 22–38% of the cases the rule labels as
  percolated: the equivalent-damage rule is systematically more permissive,
  and our cross-check bounds rather than certifies it.
- A decoupled control isolates how much of the energy channel's predictive
  advantage is constructed by the damage rule rather than discovered from the
  microstructure.
- Uncertainty is quantified end to end: microstructure-grouped cross-validation,
  split-conformal intervals, a k-nearest-neighbour applicability domain, and an
  error-propagation audit whose perturbation source is the measured spread
  between three machine-learned potentials rather than an assumed noise model.

We have tried to be explicit about what the work does not establish: the
rankings hold under a fixed synthetic-label rule, conditional conformal
coverage remains open under the frozen protocol, and no experimental
cross-validation is claimed. All data, labels and code (including the phase-
field engine) are released with the paper.

We confirm that this manuscript is original, has not been published previously,
and is not under consideration by another journal. All authors have approved
the manuscript and agree with its submission. The authors declare no competing
financial interests or personal relationships (statement included in the
manuscript), and a CRediT authorship contribution statement is provided.
No preprint version of this manuscript is available.
